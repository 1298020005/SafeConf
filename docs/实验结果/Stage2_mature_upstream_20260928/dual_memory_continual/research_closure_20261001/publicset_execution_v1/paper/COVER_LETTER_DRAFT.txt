Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, “SafeConf: Public Experimental References for Task-Level Reliability of Perturbation Predictions,” for publication in IEEE Transactions on Computational Biology and Bioinformatics.

The manuscript studies a practical problem in computational biology: ranking unreliable single-cell perturbation predictions when experimental follow-up is limited and a deployed predictor has not yet accumulated its own risk-training labels. SafeConf separates real biological experiments that construct a public reference, errors of source predictors that train a shared risk function, and subsequent target feedback that supports personalization. A weighted historical-distance identity supplies an interpretable comparator, and the evaluation distinguishes the information contributed by each component from the choice of risk learner or public representation.

The completed experiments show useful source-error supervision within the current architecture pair, including a controlled comparison with a biological-magnitude label proxy. They also identify clear limits: superiority over the strongest simple Source baseline remains unresolved, public support is a strong McFaline comparator, the frozen Orion evaluation fails its safety criterion, and the native512 scGPT/GEARS analysis remains retrospective stress evidence because all six upstream assets fail the registered competence gate. A fixed Ridge score repair corrects a mathematical output defect without resolving the transferred ordering deficits. These results support a modular method and a documented release rule that can reject an update even when biological reconstruction improves.

We believe the paper is relevant to TCBB because it combines a computational reliability method with explicit biological evidence contracts, experiment-level provenance, controlled information budgets, and reproducible evaluation. The accompanying manuscript package supplies source-linked result tables, hashes, six figures, supplementary methods, executable entry points, source snapshots, and the actual main and supplement PDF build records. The complete five-fold four-arm comparison separates historical-effect data alignment from reference architecture: pointwise neural weighting improves specific Source comparisons, whereas collection-level interaction does not establish a consistent increment. Strong simple matching references remain visible, including same-context support in McFaline. The registered fully nested readers and source-label controls further delimit which supervision and component combinations support the reliability result.
